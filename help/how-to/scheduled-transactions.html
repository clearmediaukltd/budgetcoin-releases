BudgetCoin User Guide

Scheduled transactions

A scheduled transaction repeats on its own: rent, salary, subscriptions, a regular transfer to savings. You set it up once in an account, and BudgetCoin enters it into that account each time it falls due.

The Scheduled panel

Every account has a Scheduled button in the bar above its transactions, next to Reconcile. When the account has schedules, the button shows how many there are and the next one due, for example “Scheduled · 3 next 18/11/2026 Netflix −£10.99”. Choose it to open or close the Scheduled panel. Each account remembers whether you left its panel open on this computer.

The panel has a dashed blue border because nothing in it is booked yet. Scheduled transactions don’t appear in the register, don’t change any balance and don’t affect Ready to Assign until they are entered.

Each row shows a schedule’s next date, payee, how it repeats, its category and amount. Point at a row, or move to it with the keyboard, to see what you can do with it:

  • Enter now books the next occurrence today, for example when you pay a bill early. The schedule moves on to the following date.
  • Skip this one moves on to the following date without booking anything.
  • Edit opens the schedule for changes. Changes apply to every future occurrence. To change just one occurrence, let it be entered (or choose Enter now) and then edit the transaction in the register.
  • Delete removes the schedule. Transactions it already entered stay where they are.

You can’t schedule from All accounts: schedules always belong to one account.

Adding a scheduled transaction

  1. Open the account, choose Scheduled, then Add scheduled transaction.
  2. Fill in the row:
    • First date: the first time it happens. Type dates the same way as anywhere else (see Entering dates).
    • Payee, Category, and the amount as Outflow or Inflow. For pay, choose an income category (Income for this month or Income for next month). To schedule a transfer, choose the other account under Transfers.
    • Repeats: Daily, Weekly (choose the weekday), Twice a month (choose the two days), Monthly (choose the day) or Yearly (choose the month and day). If you pick a day that a month doesn’t have, such as the 31st, it falls on that month’s last day. last day in the day list always means the end of the month.
    • Ends: Never, On a date, or After a number of times.
  3. Check the Next dates line underneath, then choose Save schedule (or press Enter). Esc cancels.

When transactions are entered

When you open a budget, everything that has fallen due since you last opened it is entered, each on its own date. If the budget stayed closed for a while, every missed occurrence is entered, and none is entered twice. Entering everything counts as one step, so Undo takes the whole lot back.

Transactions entered this way are marked New in the register, and the account’s name in the sidebar shows how many are new. A note above the register says how many were entered. Check them, then:

  • choose Mark as reviewed to clear the New marks for that account, or
  • choose Approve on a single row. Editing a transaction also clears its mark.

Transfers between accounts

A scheduled transfer belongs to the account you set it up in, but it is listed in both accounts’ panels, marked “from …” in the other account. Both sides are entered together. Only accounts in the same currency can have a scheduled transfer between them.

A scheduled transfer to or from a tracking account also needs the budget account’s category. Choose it under Category in account name, next to the Category list. The panel shows it after the transfer, for example “Transfer: Pension (Retirement)”.

Closing and deleting accounts

Closing an account pauses the schedules that use it. They are marked Paused and nothing is entered. Reopening the account starts them again from today: occurrences missed while it was closed are not entered. Deleting an account deletes its schedules, and transfers scheduled into it. Deleting a category that a schedule uses asks you where to move it, just as it does for transactions.
