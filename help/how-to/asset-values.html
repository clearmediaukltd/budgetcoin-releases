BudgetCoin User Guide

Investment and property values

The value of an investment or a house changes without any money moving. Use Set value to record what it is worth on a date. This works on investment and other asset accounts.

Setting a value

  1. Open the account and choose Set value.
  2. Enter the Date and the Value. The line beside them shows the account’s value on that date as things stand, or the value you are replacing if that date already has one.
  3. Choose Save value.

A value is the figure at the end of that day, so it includes that day’s transactions. Each date has one value, so setting a value for the same date again replaces the old one. Undo reverses it.

After that, the account is worth that value plus any money paid in or taken out after that date. For example, if you set your pension to £49,450.00 on 20 November and pay in £250.00 on 28 November, it is worth £49,700.00.

What changes

  • The top of the account shows Value and As of, the date of the latest value. If that date is more than a year ago, it says (over a year ago).
  • The sidebar, net worth (including its history) and the forecast use the value.
  • The register doesn’t change. Its Balance column still adds up the transactions, so it can differ from the value.

A value isn’t a transaction, so you can set one after the free trial ends.

Past values

Choose the As of date to see every value you have set, newest first. Edit opens Set value with that date and amount. Delete removes it. The account then goes back to the value before it, or to the total of its transactions if there is none.

Paying in and taking out

Keep entering real money as transactions on the account: contributions, employer payments and withdrawals. For a payment from your budget, enter a transfer from the budget account to the investment account and choose the category it comes from, such as Pension or Investing (see Transfers to and from tracking accounts). Money paid in from outside your budget, like an employer’s contribution, goes on the investment account with No category.

The forecast counts money paid into investment and asset accounts from outside your accounts as saving, and money taken out against it. A transfer from your budget is counted once, as part of your income minus spending. Opening balances and values don’t count.

Closing an asset account

An investment or asset account doesn’t need a zero balance to close. When you choose Close account, the app asks first. Its value becomes £0.00 from today, so it leaves your net worth from that date. Net worth before that date doesn’t change.

Past values shows the closing value with an Account closed tag. It has no Edit or Delete. Undo, or Reopen account, puts the value back.

Loans and mortgages

Debts don’t have a value to set. Enter the amount owed as the opening balance, then record payments as transfers into the loan or mortgage account. For a fixed loan, you can enter the total you will repay, interest included, as the opening balance, so the payments bring it to zero.

A house and its mortgage

Use two tracking accounts: a House (Other asset) and a Mortgage.

  • Buying. Record the loan as a transfer from the Mortgage account to the House account. For the deposit from a budget account, enter spending in a category such as House deposit, and the same amount coming into the House account with No category.
  • Valuing. Use Set value on the House account whenever you have an estimate.
  • Improvements. Enter money spent on an extension or a new kitchen as money paid into the House account. It adds to the value from that date.
  • Selling. Enter the sale money leaving the House account, then close it. Any amount left on the Mortgage account after the final payment is interest you didn’t record. Clear it with Reconcile.
