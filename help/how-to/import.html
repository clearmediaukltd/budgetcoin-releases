    User guide

    Importing bank statements

    Instead of typing every transaction, you can bring them in from a file your bank lets you download. The app reads CSV, OFX, QFX and QIF files. Your file never leaves your computer.

    Import a file

    1. Choose Import at the bottom of the sidebar.
    2. Under Import into, pick the account the statement belongs to.
    3. Choose Choose file… and select the file.
    4. For a CSV file, check which column is which (see below). For a QIF file, you may be asked for the date format.
    5. Check the preview, untick any rows you don’t want, and choose Import.

    Everything you import arrives as one step, so Undo removes the whole import if something looks wrong.

    CSV files: which column is which

    Banks lay out CSV files differently, so the app makes a best guess and shows you the first few rows to check against:

    • Date and Date format. If the dates could be read more than one way (for example 03/04/2026 could be 3 April or 4 March), the app won’t guess. Choose the format your bank uses. Example dates in the list show how each format reads.
    • Payee and Memo (both optional).
    • Amounts: either one column where money out is negative, or separate money in and money out columns. Some credit card statements show spending as a positive number; tick Spending is shown as positive for those.
    • Comma is the decimal separator for amounts written like 1.234,56.

    Rows that can’t be read, such as a “Total” line at the end of the file, are listed in the preview and left out. They’re never dropped without telling you.

    What the preview tells you

    Each row has a status:

    • New: it will be added.
    • Matches one you entered: you already typed this transaction in by hand (same amount, within 10 days). Instead of adding a copy, the app links your entry to the bank’s record and marks it cleared. Your date, payee and category are kept.
    • Already imported: it came in with an earlier import, so it’s skipped. You can import overlapping statements safely; nothing is added twice.

    For OFX and QFX files, the app also compares the bank’s balance with your cleared balance after importing, and tells you if they differ.

    If the file says it’s in a different currency from your budget, you’ll see a warning. Amounts are not converted.

    After importing: approve and categorise

    Imported transactions arrive cleared and marked To approve. They have a coloured bar on the left and an Approve button.

    • If you’ve used the same payee before, the app fills in the category you used last time.
    • Otherwise the category shows Needs a category. Until you choose one, spending counts as overspending, just like spending from an empty category, so your budget stays honest.

    To review them:

    1. On the budget screen, choose Review in the yellow banner, or open a register’s Filters and pick To approve or Needs a category under Show transactions.
    2. Edit a transaction to set its category. Saving an edit also approves it.
    3. To do many at once, tick them and use Change category… or Approve in the bar that appears.

    Tips

    • Download the same date range you’d use for reconciling, then reconcile afterwards.
    • A QIF or OFX file should hold one account. If your bank puts several accounts in one file, download them separately.
    • QIF files from investment accounts aren’t supported yet.
