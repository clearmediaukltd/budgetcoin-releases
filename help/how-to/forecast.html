BudgetCoin User Guide

Forecasting your net worth

The forecast projects your net worth 5, 10, 20 or 30 years ahead. Choose Forecast in the sidebar.

It uses your monthly saving and the growth rates under Assumptions.

The page

  • Net worth in the final year, with a low and a high figure, and your net worth today. Future amounts are rounded to whole units.
  • Monthly saving, with the income, spending and money into assets it comes from.
  • Net worth over time. The line is the expected value. The shaded band runs from poor years to good years. Point at the chart for a month’s amounts, or choose Show as a table for one row a year.
  • Milestones: the dates you pass round amounts such as £25,000, £50,000 and £100,000. If your net worth is below zero today, the first milestone is zero.
  • Goal: enter a net worth to reach. The forecast shows the expected date, plus the dates for good and poor years. Empty the box to remove the goal.

5 years, 10 years, 20 years and 30 years set how far ahead it goes. In today’s money removes inflation from future amounts.

How it’s calculated

The forecast starts from the balance of every open account today, including tracking accounts such as investments, pensions, property and loans. Then, for each month:

  1. Each account grows by its yearly rate divided by 12. An account grows only while its balance is above zero. A loan or mortgage adds interest only while money is owed.
  2. Scheduled transfers are made, such as payments into investments or towards a loan (see Scheduled transactions). Payments towards a debt stop when it reaches zero.
  3. The monthly saving is added to the chosen account. It rises with inflation each month, so it keeps its value.

Monthly saving is the average over the last 12 full months, or fewer for a newer budget, of your income minus your spending on budget accounts, plus money paid into investment and asset accounts from outside them, such as an employer’s pension contribution. Money taken out of them counts against it. Transfers between your accounts, opening balances and values you set are left out.

A pension contribution entered as a transfer from a budget account, with a category such as “Pension”, is counted once. It isn’t spending, and it’s already part of your income minus spending.

Each account starts from its value today. For an investment or asset account with a value set, the Value as of column shows that value’s date. See Investment and property values.

Assumptions

  • Monthly saving: your average. Choose Change to enter your own amount, and Use my average to go back.
  • Growth per year for each account. An empty box uses the default for its type: 2% for savings, 5% for investments, 3% for property and other assets, 0% for everything else. For a loan or mortgage, enter its interest rate. The table shows each account’s value today and in the final year. Saving goes here marks the account the monthly saving goes into. The default is your largest savings account.
  • Inflation per year. Monthly saving rises by this rate, and In today’s money uses it. Default 2.5%.

The good and poor years use each growing account’s rate plus and minus 2 percentage points. Debts and accounts at 0% stay the same.

Changes are saved in the budget. Undo reverses them.

What if…

A what-if shows a change next to the current forecast. A dashed line shows the forecast without it.

  • One-off: money out (a purchase) or money in on a date.
  • Monthly change: save more (or spend less), or save less (or spend more), each month from a date. It rises with inflation like the monthly saving.

What-ifs aren’t saved. They’re removed when you close the budget, or with Clear what-ifs.

Accounts in other currencies

Accounts in other currencies are converted at today’s exchange rate for the whole forecast. A currency with no rate is left out, and a notice says so. Choose Add a rate to enter one (see Accounts in other currencies).
