    User guide

    Seeing your net worth

    Your net worth is everything your accounts hold minus everything you owe on them. Choose Net worth in the sidebar to see it. The sidebar also shows the current total in small print next to Net worth.

    What the page shows

    • Net worth, assets and debts today. An account with money in it counts as an asset. An account below zero counts as a debt. So an overdrawn current account is a debt, and a credit card you’ve overpaid is an asset.
    • Over time. One bar per month: assets above the line, debts below, with your net worth drawn as a line. Each month is valued on its last day, and the current month is valued today. Choose 12 months, 24 months or All; the page opens on your last choice. Point at a month to see exact amounts, or choose Show as a table to read the same numbers as a table.
    • By account type. Each type (current accounts, savings, credit cards, loans…) with its total, and each account’s value. Choose an account’s name to open its register.

    Budget accounts and tracking accounts both count. Today’s figures include transactions dated in the future, so each account matches its balance in the sidebar. In the history, a future transaction counts from the month it’s dated. A closed account only appears if it still has a balance.

    An investment or asset account with a value set counts at that value, plus the money paid in or taken out after it. See Investment and property values.

    Accounts in other currencies

    Accounts in another currency are converted into your budget’s currency using the exchange rates stored in the budget (see Accounts in other currencies). Each month uses the rate for that day: the latest rate on or before it, or the earliest one if all your rates are newer. The breakdown shows the account’s own balance and the rate used.

    If a currency has no rate at all, those accounts are left out of the totals and a notice says so. Choose Add a rate to enter one.

    Net worth only reads your accounts. Looking at it never changes your budget.
