    User guide

    Accounts in other currencies

    Your budget has one currency, chosen when you created it (see Settings). Your categories, Ready to Assign and reports are all in that currency. Individual accounts can be in other currencies, for example a euro account in a budget kept in pounds.

    Add an account in another currency

    1. Choose Add account in the sidebar.
    2. Pick the account’s Currency.
    3. Check the exchange rate: how much one unit of the account’s currency is worth in your budget’s currency today. For a euro account in a pounds budget, if €1 is worth £0.85, the rate is 0.85.
      • If the budget already has a rate for that currency (from another account in it), that rate is filled in.
      • Otherwise, choose Fetch rate to download today’s European Central Bank rate. If Update exchange rates when this budget opens is on in Settings → Exchange rates, the rate is downloaded as soon as you pick the currency.
      • The line under the rate says where it came from. You can change it, or type your own if the European Central Bank doesn’t publish that currency.
    4. Enter the balance in the account’s own currency, then choose Add account.

    The starting rate is saved as a rate you entered, on the balance date.

    The account’s balance and transactions are shown in its own currency, everywhere.

    Keeping rates up to date

    When a budget with accounts in other currencies opens, the app downloads the European Central Bank’s latest daily rates (once a day) and uses them as the default rate for new transactions. You can also choose Update rates now in Settings → Exchange rates, or turn automatic updates off there.

    The exchange rates icon (two arrows) next to the budget name, at the top of the sidebar, shows when rates were last downloaded: point at it, or move to it with the keyboard, to see the date. It’s green when rates were downloaded today and grey when they’re older, and it spins while downloading. Select it to open the exchange rate settings.

    • Rates you type yourself are never replaced.
    • Rates are published on working days, around mid-afternoon Central European Time.
    • About 30 major currencies are covered. For others, type the rate yourself when you enter a transaction.
    • Downloaded rates only affect new transactions. Transactions you’ve already saved keep their own rate.

    Viewing and editing rates

    Choose Settings → Exchange rates, or select the exchange rates icon next to the budget name. Each currency your accounts use shows its current rate (for example 1 EUR = 0.8712 GBP), where it came from, and its earlier rates, newest first.

    • Enter a rate: type a date and the rate (how much of your budget’s currency one unit is worth), then choose Save rate. Entering a rate for a day that already has one replaces it, including a downloaded rate.
    • Edit: choose Edit next to a rate to copy it into the form, change it, and save.
    • Delete: rates you entered can be deleted. Downloaded rates can’t (the next download would bring them back), but you can replace them by entering your own.
    • All of these can be undone with ⌘Z on a Mac or Ctrl+Z on Windows and Linux.

    If the European Central Bank doesn’t publish one of your currencies, enter its rates here; new transactions in that currency then pick them up.

    Transactions

    Type amounts in the account’s currency. Below the row, an Exchange rate box shows the rate the transaction will be converted at. It’s filled in with the latest rate you’ve used for that currency. Change it if your bank used a different rate. Each rate you type is remembered for that date and becomes the new default.

    Once saved, a transaction keeps its rate. Editing it later doesn’t re-convert it at today’s rate, so last month’s budget never changes behind your back. To correct a rate, edit the transaction and change it. When you edit a transaction, Booked as shows what it counted for in your budget’s currency. Point at an amount in the register to see the same thing.

    Transfers between currencies

    When you transfer between two accounts in different currencies, enter the amount that leaves this account, then fill in Amount in (other currency): what arrived in the other account. The two sides count as the same value in your budget, so a transfer never changes Ready to Assign.

    Importing

    Bank statements for an account in another currency are read in that currency, and each transaction is converted at the latest rate you’ve entered. Enter at least one transaction (or the account’s starting balance) with a rate first.
