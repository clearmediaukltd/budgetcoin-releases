BudgetCoin User Guide

Introduction

This is an envelope budgeting app that keeps your data on your computer. Every penny is planned before you spend it, your budget files can be password-protected, and you can track your full net worth, including investments, in any currency.

Your first budget

When you open the app you’ll see the Welcome screen. It lists your budgets, most recently opened first: the ones in your budgets folder plus any you’ve opened from somewhere else (shown with their folder name). Choose a card to open it, or New budget to create one. If a file has moved or been deleted, its card says File not found, and its × takes it off the list.

Choose the app’s language

The language button is in the top-right corner of the Welcome screen. It shows the current language’s flag and name. Choose it to pick another language. The app remembers your choice on this computer. Until you choose, it uses your computer’s language if the app has it, and English otherwise.

The app is available in English, Dutch (Nederlands), French (Français), German (Deutsch), Italian (Italiano), Portuguese (Português) and Spanish (Español). A computer set to one of these languages starts in it, whatever the region (for example Canada, Belgium, Mexico, Austria or Switzerland). Portuguese uses Brazilian wording, and Portugal also gets it. This user guide is in English only for now.

Try the sample budget

Choose Try a sample budget to explore before setting up your own. It creates a budget called “Sample budget” with three months of example activity ending today: a current account, savings, a credit card and a pension, bills and everyday categories, a split transaction and a flagged one. It uses your computer’s currency and formats.

The sample is an ordinary budget. Change anything you like, and delete it when you’re done (see Delete a budget). Choosing Try a sample budget again reopens the same sample, with any changes you made. To start the sample afresh, delete it and try it again. The example names are in English for now.

Create a budget

  1. Choose Create a new budget.
  2. Give it a name, for example “Household”.
  3. Check the currency, date format and number format. They’re filled in from your computer’s region settings. The Preview line shows how dates and amounts will look. These settings are stored inside the budget, so it looks the same on any computer.
  4. Optionally tick Protect this budget with a password. The file is then encrypted (see Privacy & security).
  5. Choose Create budget.

If you set a password, the app shows your recovery key once. It’s the only way to open the budget if you forget the password. Copy it into a password manager or write it down, tick the box to confirm, then continue.

When the budget opens, the setup tour starts (see below).

Budgets are saved in a BudgetCoin folder in your Documents folder. There is no Save button: changes are saved automatically a moment after you make them. If saving ever fails, a message appears at the top of the window.

The setup tour

A new budget starts with a short tour that shows you, one step at a time, how to set it up. Each step moves on by itself once you’ve done what it asks; the last one just shows you around. The rest of the window dims, a ring highlights the control to use, and a large tip beside it explains what to do:

  1. Add your first account. Choose Add account in the sidebar. The tour moves on by itself once the account is saved.
  2. Start with a group. Open the ⋯ menu at the top of the Category column, choose Add group and give it a name, such as “Bills”. The tour moves on by itself once the group is added.
  3. Add categories to your group. Choose ＋ at the end of the group’s row and type a name. The tour moves on once it’s added. You can add more categories and groups any time.
  4. Assign all your money. Type amounts into Assigned. The tip shows how much is still to assign, and the tour moves on when it reaches zero. (If there’s nothing to assign, choose Next.)
  5. Record money in and out. The tour opens your account so you can enter a transaction. When you’re paid, enter it as an Inflow with the category Income for this month: it goes into Ready to Assign. (The balance you entered in step 1 is already there, so don’t enter this month’s pay again.) The tour moves on once you’ve entered a transaction.
  6. See your budget. The tour goes back to the budget and highlights the Available amount of the category you just spent from. Choose Finish to end the tour.

The tour only points the way. Everything you do during it is a normal change, so Undo works as usual. You can click anywhere in the window while the tour is showing; if you wander off, choose Show me to go back to the step.

Choose Skip tour (or press Esc while the tip has focus) to close it. If you quit part-way, the tour carries on from the same step next time you open the budget. To take it again any time, go to Help and choose Take the setup tour. The sample budget doesn’t start the tour.

Add your accounts

Choose Add account in the sidebar. Give the account a name, pick its type and enter its current balance and the date of that balance. For a credit card or loan, enter what you owe as a negative amount by typing a minus sign first (for example -25000 for −£250.00). You can type sums here too (see Typing amounts and sums).

  • Budget accounts (current, savings, cash, credit cards) count towards Ready to Assign.
  • Tracking accounts (investments, property, loans) only show a balance. Untick On budget to make any account a tracking account.

To record spending and income, select the account in the sidebar. See Entering transactions.

Credit cards work like your other budget accounts:

  • Spending on the card comes out of its category, just like paying cash.
  • Paying the card off is a transfer from your current account (choose Transfer: card name as the category). It doesn’t change your budget: the money already left your categories when you spent it.
  • Debt the card had when you added it is money you’ve already spent, so it comes out of Ready to Assign. If that makes Ready to Assign negative, take money back from some categories until you’ve caught up.
  • If you overspend a category on the card, it’s treated like any other overspending (see below).

Close or delete an account. At the top of an account, choose ⋯ (Account actions) next to its type. Close account takes it off the sidebar’s main list but keeps its history (find it under Closed accounts; it needs a zero balance). Delete account… removes the account and all its transactions, after telling you how many. Transfers to other accounts aren’t lost: they stay in those accounts as ordinary transactions marked Needs a category, so their balances don’t change. Undo brings a deleted account back.

Add groups and categories

Categories are what your money is for, like “Rent” or “Groceries”. Groups keep related categories together, like “Bills” or “Everyday”.

  • Open the ⋯ menu at the top of the Category column (on the same line as Collapse all) and choose Add group. A new row appears after your last group: type a name and press Enter, or click away to add it. Esc cancels.
  • Point at a group’s row (or move to it with the keyboard) and choose ＋ at the end of its name to add a category to it.
  • Select a group’s or category’s name to rename, hide or delete it. Hidden categories keep their money and still count towards your totals; they’re just out of the way. To see them again, choose Show hidden categories in the ⋯ menu (it shows how many are hidden, and a tick while they are shown), then select one and choose Show again.
  • Deleting a category that has transactions or assigned money asks you to choose another category to move them to; assigned amounts are added to that category’s, month by month, so Ready to Assign doesn’t change. A group can be deleted once it’s empty. Undo reverses any delete.

Arrange your budget

  • Reorder categories: drag a category’s name up or down. Drop it in another group to move it there, or onto a group’s row to make it that group’s first category. Only its place changes, never its money.
  • Reorder groups: drag a group by its name.
  • From the keyboard: select a name with Tab, then press ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ (Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ on Windows) to move it one place. A category at the top or bottom of its group moves into the next group.
  • Collapse a group with the arrow before its name to hide its categories; select it again to expand. Collapse all / Expand all, just above the first group, (or ⌥-click, Alt-click, on any group’s arrow) does every group at once. Each group’s row always shows its totals for every month (assigned, activity and available, including hidden categories), so a collapsed group still shows its money. The budget remembers which groups are collapsed.
  • Reorder accounts in the sidebar the same way: drag an account, or use ⌥↑ / ⌥↓. Accounts move within their own list (budget or tracking).
  • Collapse the sidebar’s lists by selecting Budget accounts or Tracking accounts. This computer remembers which are collapsed.
  • Widen the sidebar by dragging its right edge (or focus the edge and use ← / →) so long account names fit; double-click the edge for the standard width. Hover over a name to see it in full.
  • Reordering can be undone like any other change.

Assign your money

The top of each month shows Ready to Assign: money you have that hasn’t been assigned to a category yet. Select a category’s Assigned amount, type how much to give it, and press Enter. Ready to Assign goes down as you assign. Aim for zero. If it goes below zero the header says Overassigned and you should take money back from a category.

Below it, the header shows how that figure adds up:

  • Left over from last month: money that wasn’t assigned last month.
  • Income for this month: income marked Income for this month this month, plus anything marked Income for next month last month.
  • Overspent in last month: cash spending that went over a category’s budget comes out of this month.
  • Assigned in this month: money you’ve assigned to categories this month.

Money you assign in a later month doesn’t change this month’s Ready to Assign: it comes out of that later month’s. If you plan ahead further than your money goes, the later month shows Overassigned.

If you’ve marked income as Income for next month, an Income held for line shows how much is waiting for next month. It’s in your accounts but not yet in Ready to Assign. In 6-month view, point at Ready to Assign to see the same breakdown.

The Available column shows what’s left in each category. A negative amount, shown in orange, means the category is overspent. Assign more to it, or it comes out of next month’s Ready to Assign (see Overspending).

Auto-assign a month

Instead of filling in every category by hand, choose Auto-assign next to a month’s name. Each option shows how much it would assign and how many categories it changes:

  • Assigned last month or Spent last month: repeat last month.
  • Average assigned or Average spent: the average over the last 3, 6 or 12 months (choose in the menu).
  • Reset available to zero: takes back money sitting unspent in categories, returning it to Ready to Assign.
  • Reset assigned to zero: clears the month’s assignments.

It applies to every visible category, as one step: Undo reverses the whole thing.

Move money between categories

Plans change. To move money, change the Assigned amounts: lower the category you’re taking it from and raise the one it goes to. You can type a sum, such as -50 to take £50 off what’s there or +50 to add it (see Typing amounts and sums). Lowering a category without raising another puts the money back in Ready to Assign.

See what makes up a category’s activity

Select a category’s Activity amount (underlined) to see the transactions behind it that month, newest first: date, payee, account and amount. For a split transaction, only the part that went to this category is shown, marked Split. The Total at the bottom is always the same as the Activity amount. Press Esc or click elsewhere to close the list.

Choose Show in All accounts to open those transactions in the All accounts register, where you can change, search or export them (see Searching and filtering).

Use the arrows next to the month name (or ⌘[ and ⌘], Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] on Windows and Linux) to move between months, and 1 month / 2 months / 3 months / 6 months to choose how many to see at once. With 6 months, each month shows a compact column: the assigned amount (select it to change it) next to what’s available. Point at a cell to see its activity, or switch back to 3 months or fewer for the full Assigned, Activity and Available columns. Under each column heading is that month’s total for the column (in 6-month view, total assigned · total available). In 6-month view, the ✦ button next to each month opens Auto-assign. Undo and Redo work for every change until you close the budget (or it locks). Closing clears the undo history, so a budget you open again starts with nothing to undo.

The current month is outlined in blue and tagged This month. Earlier months are greyed and tagged Completed, but you can still change them. Later months are tagged Planning.

Open and exit budgets

Choose Exit budget in the top bar to go back to the Welcome screen, then pick another budget from the list. A password-protected budget asks for its password. If you’ve forgotten it, choose Use my recovery key instead.

If the app closed before your last changes were saved, they’re recovered the next time you open the budget.

Delete a budget

On the Welcome screen, point at a budget and choose the bin icon. Inside a budget, go to Settings → General and choose Delete budget… at the bottom.

To confirm, type the budget’s name exactly. A password-protected budget also asks for its password (or tick Use the recovery key instead). The file then moves to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows), so you can still restore it from there. Restored files show up on the Welcome screen again.

A budget on a network drive or some USB drives can’t go to the Trash: there it’s deleted for good. Copies you’ve exported or backed up elsewhere are never touched.

For a budget stored outside your budgets folder, the same dialog offers Just remove from this list, which forgets the budget but leaves the file alone. A budget whose file can’t be found shows × instead, which only removes it from the list.

The envelope method

  1. Money arrives in Ready to Assign.
  2. You assign it to categories, like putting cash into envelopes.
  3. Spending comes out of its category. If a category is overspent, move money from another one (see Overspending).
  4. Whatever is left in a category rolls into next month.

You only budget money you already have.

Entering transactions

Select an account in the sidebar to open its register: every transaction in that account, newest first, with the balance after each one.

Add a transaction

  1. Select the entry row at the top of the list (or press ⌘N, Ctrl+N on Windows and Linux). It’s the tinted row marked +, with an Add button at the end, and it’s always there, ready for the next transaction.
  2. Fill it in, using Tab to move between fields:
    • Date: type the digits in your budget’s date format, or choose the calendar button (Alt+↓). ↑/↓ move a day (see Entering dates).
    • Payee: who you paid or who paid you. Payees you’ve used before are suggested as you type, and picking one fills in the category you used last time (see Managing payees).
    • Category: which envelope the money comes from. For money coming in, choose one of the Income options so you can assign it on the budget:
      • Income for this month adds it to this month’s Ready to Assign straight away.
      • Income for next month holds it back until next month. Use it to live on last month’s income: pay that arrives late in November is budgeted in December, so you can fill December’s categories as soon as it lands without it showing up as unassigned money in November.
    • Memo (optional).
    • Outflow for money leaving the account, or Inflow for money coming in. Sums work (see Typing amounts and sums).
    • Cleared: select the round button if the transaction has already shown up on your bank statement.
  3. Press Enter (from any field, including the Category list) or choose Add.

The new transaction takes its place in the list below, by date, and is highlighted and tagged Added for a few seconds. If its date puts it further down, the list scrolls to it. If your search or filters hide it, a message tells you so.

The entry row empties, keeping the same date, ready for the next one. Clicking somewhere else also adds what you’ve typed. To start again, press Esc or choose ✕ to clear the row.

To spread one payment over several categories, choose Split into several categories… in the Category list. Each line gets its own category, memo and amount.

Transfers between accounts

To move money between two of your accounts, choose Transfer: account name as the category. The app adds the matching transaction to the other account automatically, and changing or deleting either side changes both. Transfers between two budget accounts don’t need a category, because the money stays in your budget.

Transfers to and from tracking accounts

Money that leaves your budget for a tracking account, such as a payment into a pension or a mortgage payment, comes out of a category. When you choose a transfer to or from a tracking account, a second list, Category in account name, appears below the row. Choose the budget category the money comes from, for example Retirement. Money coming back from a tracking account can go to a category, or to Income for this month or Income for next month so you can assign it again.

It works the same whichever account you enter it from. The budget account’s register shows the category; the tracking account’s register shows Transfer.

Tracking accounts

Transactions in tracking accounts (investments, property, loans) don’t use categories, because they aren’t part of your budget. Leave the category as No category, or choose a transfer.

Edit, delete and clear

  • Edit a transaction where it is: select its row (or move to it with ↑ and ↓ and press Enter). The row’s fields become editable. Press Enter or choose Save to save, or Esc to cancel. Clicking anywhere else, including another transaction, also saves your changes. If something’s missing, the row stays open and says what.
  • Delete removes it. Use Undo if you change your mind. If the transaction is reconciled (or it’s a transfer whose other side is), the app asks first, because deleting it means the account no longer agrees with your bank statement. This applies to Delete on selected transactions too.
  • The round button marks a transaction Cleared (it shows C) or not cleared. Reconciled transactions show R (see Reconciling an account).

Flags

Flags mark transactions you want to come back to, like “Tax” or “Refund due”. Select the flag button on a row and choose a colour, or Remove flag. You can also flag a transaction while you type it in the new-transaction row at the top: the flag is added with it. A flagged transaction shows its colour and name. To show only flagged transactions, one flag, or unflagged ones, use Filters in the search row (see Searching and filtering).

All accounts

All accounts in the sidebar lists every transaction from every account, newest first, with an Account column. The balance on each row is the balance of that row’s account. You can add transactions here too: choose the account first.

Change many transactions at once

Tick the box at the start of each transaction you want to change, or tick the box in the header to select every transaction shown (search or filter first to narrow the list; see Searching and filtering). A bar appears at the bottom of the register, over the list, so nothing moves. It has:

  • Mark cleared / Mark uncleared
  • Flag… to flag them all, or remove their flags
  • Change category… to move them all to one category
  • Delete

Each action changes all the selected transactions together, and one Undo reverses it. Some transactions can’t be changed this way and are left as they were: reconciled ones can’t be uncleared, and split, transfer and tracking-account transactions keep their categories. The app tells you how many were left.

Split a transaction across categories

One payment can cover several categories, like a supermarket shop with groceries and household items. In the Category list, choose Split into several categories…. Each line has its own category, memo and amount. Choose Add line for more, or Remove to drop one. The transaction’s total is the sum of its lines. A split can’t include a transfer.

Rename or close an account

Open the account, choose ⋯ (Account actions) at the top, then Rename. To stop using an account, bring its balance to zero, then choose Close account from the same menu. Closed accounts keep their history and are listed under Closed accounts in the sidebar, where you can open one and choose Reopen account.

Overspending

A category is overspent when more has gone out of it than it had. Its Available amount turns negative and orange.

Fix it

Assign more to the overspent category. Select its Assigned amount and type the extra, for example +50. If Ready to Assign is empty, take the money from another category the same way, with -50 on its Assigned amount.

If you leave it

At the start of the next month the category goes back to zero, and the overspent amount comes out of that month’s Ready to Assign. The month header shows it as Overspent in month. This works the same for spending on a credit card.

Carry overspending forward

Some categories are better left negative until you fill them, for example a reimbursable expense you’ll be paid back for, or a bill you pay ahead and budget for over the next few months. For these, keep the overspending in the category: in the month where it should start, select the arrow next to the category’s Available amount. The arrow shows when you point at the row or move to it with the keyboard. It saves straight away.

From that month on, an overspent balance stays in the category and is carried into the next month as a negative amount. It doesn’t come out of Ready to Assign. Money you assign later fills it up first. The arrow stays shown, in orange, in every month where the category carries its overspending forward.

Turning it on or off changes that month and every month after it. The setting stays on until you turn it off. To stop, point at the arrow in the month where it should stop: it turns into ✕. Select it. That month’s overspending then comes out of the next month’s Ready to Assign again. Undo reverses the change.

Scheduled transactions

A scheduled transaction repeats on its own: rent, salary, subscriptions, a regular transfer to savings. You set it up once in an account, and BudgetCoin enters it into that account each time it falls due.

The Scheduled panel

Every account has a Scheduled button in the bar above its transactions, next to Reconcile. When the account has schedules, the button shows how many there are and the next one due, for example “Scheduled · 3 next 18/11/2026 Netflix −£10.99”. Choose it to open or close the Scheduled panel. Each account remembers whether you left its panel open on this computer.

The panel has a dashed blue border because nothing in it is booked yet. Scheduled transactions don’t appear in the register, don’t change any balance and don’t affect Ready to Assign until they are entered.

Each row shows a schedule’s next date, payee, how it repeats, its category and amount. Point at a row, or move to it with the keyboard, to see what you can do with it:

  • Enter now books the next occurrence today, for example when you pay a bill early. The schedule moves on to the following date.
  • Skip this one moves on to the following date without booking anything.
  • Edit opens the schedule for changes. Changes apply to every future occurrence. To change just one occurrence, let it be entered (or choose Enter now) and then edit the transaction in the register.
  • Delete removes the schedule. Transactions it already entered stay where they are.

You can’t schedule from All accounts: schedules always belong to one account.

Adding a scheduled transaction

  1. Open the account, choose Scheduled, then Add scheduled transaction.
  2. Fill in the row:
    • First date: the first time it happens. Type dates the same way as anywhere else (see Entering dates).
    • Payee, Category, and the amount as Outflow or Inflow. For pay, choose an income category (Income for this month or Income for next month). To schedule a transfer, choose the other account under Transfers.
    • Repeats: Daily, Weekly (choose the weekday), Twice a month (choose the two days), Monthly (choose the day) or Yearly (choose the month and day). If you pick a day that a month doesn’t have, such as the 31st, it falls on that month’s last day. last day in the day list always means the end of the month.
    • Ends: Never, On a date, or After a number of times.
  3. Check the Next dates line underneath, then choose Save schedule (or press Enter). Esc cancels.

When transactions are entered

When you open a budget, everything that has fallen due since you last opened it is entered, each on its own date. If the budget stayed closed for a while, every missed occurrence is entered, and none is entered twice. Entering everything counts as one step, so Undo takes the whole lot back.

Transactions entered this way are marked New in the register, and the account’s name in the sidebar shows how many are new. A note above the register says how many were entered. Check them, then:

  • choose Mark as reviewed to clear the New marks for that account, or
  • choose Approve on a single row. Editing a transaction also clears its mark.

Transfers between accounts

A scheduled transfer belongs to the account you set it up in, but it is listed in both accounts’ panels, marked “from …” in the other account. Both sides are entered together. Only accounts in the same currency can have a scheduled transfer between them.

A scheduled transfer to or from a tracking account also needs the budget account’s category. Choose it under Category in account name, next to the Category list. The panel shows it after the transfer, for example “Transfer: Pension (Retirement)”.

Closing and deleting accounts

Closing an account pauses the schedules that use it. They are marked Paused and nothing is entered. Reopening the account starts them again from today: occurrences missed while it was closed are not entered. Deleting an account deletes its schedules, and transfers scheduled into it. Deleting a category that a schedule uses asks you where to move it, just as it does for transactions.

Searching and filtering transactions

Every register, and All accounts, has a search row above the transactions. Use it to find a payment, check what you spent somewhere, or narrow the list before changing or exporting transactions.

Search

Type in the search box (or press ⌘F on a Mac, Ctrl+F on Windows and Linux, to jump to it). The list shrinks as you type. Search looks in:

  • the payee
  • the memo, including the memos on each line of a split
  • the category, including each category of a split
  • the account name, on All accounts

Capitals and accents don’t matter: cafe finds Café. Matching text is highlighted in the list.

You can also type an amount, the way your budget writes numbers (for example 50, 1,234.50, or 1.234,50 if your budget uses a comma for decimals). It finds that exact amount, money in or out, including the lines of a split. 50 finds 50.00 but not 5.00 or 150.00. A currency symbol or minus sign makes no difference.

Press Esc in the search box, or the × at its end, to clear it.

Filters

Choose Filters to narrow the list further:

  • Date: This month, Last month, Last 3 months, This year, or Custom with your own from and to dates.
  • Status: not cleared, cleared, or reconciled.
  • Show transactions: flagged, one flag colour, not flagged, To approve, or Needs a category.

The button shows how many filters are on, for example Filters · 2, and is tinted while any are. Reset filters in the panel turns them all off.

Hide reconciled transactions

Select the lock at the top of the cleared column to hide every reconciled transaction. While they’re hidden, the lock has a line through it and is tinted. Select it again to show them. Clear all leaves this setting as it is.

A category from the budget

When you choose Show in All accounts from a category’s Activity list on the budget, All accounts opens showing just that category’s transactions for that month: the date filter is set to the month, and a Category chip (for example Category: Groceries) appears next to Filters. Transactions on tracking accounts aren’t included, because they don’t count towards the budget. Select the × on the chip to show every category again, or Clear all to remove the month as well.

What you see

On the right of the search row, the count shows how many transactions are listed: 248 transactions, or 3 of 248 while a search or filter is on. Clear all removes the search and every filter.

Searching and filtering only hide rows. They never change a balance: each row’s balance is still the account’s real running balance.

Scheduled transactions waiting in the Scheduled panel aren’t searched. Ones that have already been entered are ordinary transactions and are.

Act on the results

  • Change many at once: tick the box in the header to select every transaction showing, then use the bar that appears (see Entering transactions).
  • Export: Export to CSV saves the transactions showing to a spreadsheet file. While a search or filter is on it says, for example, Export 3 to CSV, and saves just those. The file is the same as the transactions export in Settings (see Exporting and backing up).

Managing payees

A payee is who you paid or who paid you. The app adds one the first time you type or import a new name. Over time you can end up with several names for the same shop, especially from bank imports: Tesco, TESCO STORES 2231, Tesco Express. The Payees page is where you tidy them up.

Open it from Settings → Payees.

What the list shows

Each payee has:

  • its name, with Hidden next to it if you’ve hidden it
  • Also matches: the other names merged into it (see Merge payees)
  • Transactions: how many transactions use it, plus any scheduled transactions, for example 12 + 1 scheduled
  • Last category: the category its most recent transaction went to. Split transactions and transfers don’t count.

Account names used for transfers aren’t listed. They change when you rename the account.

Type in Filter payees to find a payee by its name or by one of its other names.

Rename a payee

Click the name, type the new one, then press Enter or click away. Esc puts the old name back. Every transaction with that payee shows the new name.

The old name is kept under Also matches:, so if your bank keeps sending TESCO STORES 2231 after you’ve renamed it to Tesco, the next import still finds Tesco. Changing only capitals (tesco to Tesco) doesn’t keep the old spelling. Choose the × beside an old name if you don’t want it to match any more.

If you type a name another payee already has, or one that already finds another payee, the app offers to merge the two instead (see below). Choose Cancel to keep both as they were.

Merge payees

  1. Tick two or more payees.
  2. Choose Merge….
  3. Pick the name to keep, then choose Merge into….

All their transactions and scheduled transactions move to the payee you kept. The other names are remembered under Also matches:, so when you type one of them, or a bank import uses it, the app picks the payee you kept instead of adding the old name again.

To stop a name matching, choose the × beside it. The next time that name is typed or imported, it becomes a payee of its own again.

Hide a payee

Hide takes a payee out of the suggestions you see while typing, for example a shop that has closed. Its transactions don’t change. Show brings it back. If you type a hidden payee’s name in full, the app still uses that payee.

Delete a payee

Delete only appears for payees that no transaction or scheduled transaction uses. To get rid of a payee that’s still used, merge it into another one, or hide it.

The category is filled in for you

When you type or pick a payee you’ve used before, the app fills in the category from that payee’s last transaction straight away, so you can press Enter to save from the Payee box. It only does this while Category is empty or still holds a category it filled in, so changing the payee changes that category too. A category you chose yourself is never replaced. Bank imports fill in the category the same way.

Undo

Every rename, merge, hide, delete and × can be undone with ⌘Z on a Mac or Ctrl+Z on Windows and Linux. Undoing a merge brings back every payee involved, and their transactions, in one step.

Importing bank statements

Instead of typing every transaction, you can bring them in from a file your bank lets you download. The app reads CSV, OFX, QFX and QIF files. Your file never leaves your computer.

Import a file

  1. Choose Import at the bottom of the sidebar.
  2. Under Import into, pick the account the statement belongs to.
  3. Choose Choose file… and select the file.
  4. For a CSV file, check which column is which (see below). For a QIF file, you may be asked for the date format.
  5. Check the preview, untick any rows you don’t want, and choose Import.

Everything you import arrives as one step, so Undo removes the whole import if something looks wrong.

CSV files: which column is which

Banks lay out CSV files differently, so the app makes a best guess and shows you the first few rows to check against:

  • Date and Date format. If the dates could be read more than one way (for example 03/04/2026 could be 3 April or 4 March), the app won’t guess. Choose the format your bank uses. Example dates in the list show how each format reads.
  • Payee and Memo (both optional).
  • Amounts: either one column where money out is negative, or separate money in and money out columns. Some credit card statements show spending as a positive number; tick Spending is shown as positive for those.
  • Comma is the decimal separator for amounts written like 1.234,56.

Rows that can’t be read, such as a “Total” line at the end of the file, are listed in the preview and left out. They’re never dropped without telling you.

What the preview tells you

Each row has a status:

  • New: it will be added.
  • Matches one you entered: you already typed this transaction in by hand (same amount, within 10 days). Instead of adding a copy, the app links your entry to the bank’s record and marks it cleared. Your date, payee and category are kept.
  • Already imported: it came in with an earlier import, so it’s skipped. You can import overlapping statements safely; nothing is added twice.

For OFX and QFX files, the app also compares the bank’s balance with your cleared balance after importing, and tells you if they differ.

If the file says it’s in a different currency from your budget, you’ll see a warning. Amounts are not converted.

After importing: approve and categorise

Imported transactions arrive cleared and marked To approve. They have a coloured bar on the left and an Approve button.

  • If you’ve used the same payee before, the app fills in the category you used last time.
  • Otherwise the category shows Needs a category. Until you choose one, spending counts as overspending, just like spending from an empty category, so your budget stays honest.

To review them:

  1. On the budget screen, choose Review in the yellow banner, or open a register’s Filters and pick To approve or Needs a category under Show transactions.
  2. Edit a transaction to set its category. Saving an edit also approves it.
  3. To do many at once, tick them and use Change category… or Approve in the bar that appears.

Tips

  • Download the same date range you’d use for reconciling, then reconcile afterwards.
  • A QIF or OFX file should hold one account. If your bank puts several accounts in one file, download them separately.
  • QIF files from investment accounts aren’t supported yet.

Reconciling an account

Reconciling checks that the app agrees with your bank, so small mistakes don’t build up. Do it whenever you get a statement, or every week or two.

Before you start

Open the account. At the top you’ll see three balances:

  • Cleared: transactions that have shown up at your bank (marked with C, or R once reconciled).
  • Uncleared: transactions you’ve entered that the bank hasn’t processed yet.
  • Working balance: the two together.

Go through your bank’s list and use the round button on each matching transaction to mark it cleared.

Reconcile

  1. Choose Reconcile.
  2. The app shows your cleared balance. If your bank shows the same amount, choose Yes, that’s what my bank shows. You’re done.
  3. If not, choose No, it’s different, enter the balance your bank shows and the statement date, and choose Check. For a credit card or loan, enter what you owe as a negative amount.
  4. If there’s still a difference, look for transactions you haven’t entered or haven’t marked cleared, and fix them. Or choose Add adjustment and finish to add a “Reconciliation adjustment” transaction for the difference. In a budget account the adjustment goes to Ready to Assign, so check your budget afterwards.

When you finish, every cleared transaction becomes reconciled (R). Reconciled transactions are the part of your history you’ve confirmed against the bank. Undo reverses a reconcile if you need to. Deleting a reconciled transaction asks you to confirm first, since it would put the account out of step with the bank.

Reconciled transactions are locked

A reconciled transaction shows a lock beside its R. You can still change its memo (select the row and type) and its flag, but nothing else: not the date, payee, category, amount or account, so the account can’t drift away from the statement by accident. A transfer is locked when either side is reconciled. Bulk edits leave locked transactions as they are and say how many they skipped.

To change one anyway, select its R and choose Unlock. It goes back to cleared, so your cleared balance doesn’t change, and you can edit it. The next reconcile locks it again. Undo reverses an unlock. For a transfer locked by its other side, unlock it in the other account.

Accounts in other currencies

Your budget has one currency, chosen when you created it (see Settings). Your categories, Ready to Assign and reports are all in that currency. Individual accounts can be in other currencies, for example a euro account in a budget kept in pounds.

Add an account in another currency

  1. Choose Add account in the sidebar.
  2. Pick the account’s Currency.
  3. Check the exchange rate: how much one unit of the account’s currency is worth in your budget’s currency today. For a euro account in a pounds budget, if €1 is worth £0.85, the rate is 0.85.
    • If the budget already has a rate for that currency (from another account in it), that rate is filled in.
    • Otherwise, choose Fetch rate to download today’s European Central Bank rate. If Update exchange rates when this budget opens is on in Settings → Exchange rates, the rate is downloaded as soon as you pick the currency.
    • The line under the rate says where it came from. You can change it, or type your own if the European Central Bank doesn’t publish that currency.
  4. Enter the balance in the account’s own currency, then choose Add account.

The starting rate is saved as a rate you entered, on the balance date.

The account’s balance and transactions are shown in its own currency, everywhere.

Keeping rates up to date

When a budget with accounts in other currencies opens, the app downloads the European Central Bank’s latest daily rates (once a day) and uses them as the default rate for new transactions. You can also choose Update rates now in Settings → Exchange rates, or turn automatic updates off there.

The exchange rates icon (two arrows) next to the budget name, at the top of the sidebar, shows when rates were last downloaded: point at it, or move to it with the keyboard, to see the date. It’s green when rates were downloaded today and grey when they’re older, and it spins while downloading. Select it to open the exchange rate settings.

  • Rates you type yourself are never replaced.
  • Rates are published on working days, around mid-afternoon Central European Time.
  • About 30 major currencies are covered. For others, type the rate yourself when you enter a transaction.
  • Downloaded rates only affect new transactions. Transactions you’ve already saved keep their own rate.

Viewing and editing rates

Choose Settings → Exchange rates, or select the exchange rates icon next to the budget name. Each currency your accounts use shows its current rate (for example 1 EUR = 0.8712 GBP), where it came from, and its earlier rates, newest first.

  • Enter a rate: type a date and the rate (how much of your budget’s currency one unit is worth), then choose Save rate. Entering a rate for a day that already has one replaces it, including a downloaded rate.
  • Edit: choose Edit next to a rate to copy it into the form, change it, and save.
  • Delete: rates you entered can be deleted. Downloaded rates can’t (the next download would bring them back), but you can replace them by entering your own.
  • All of these can be undone with ⌘Z on a Mac or Ctrl+Z on Windows and Linux.

If the European Central Bank doesn’t publish one of your currencies, enter its rates here; new transactions in that currency then pick them up.

Transactions

Type amounts in the account’s currency. Below the row, an Exchange rate box shows the rate the transaction will be converted at. It’s filled in with the latest rate you’ve used for that currency. Change it if your bank used a different rate. Each rate you type is remembered for that date and becomes the new default.

Once saved, a transaction keeps its rate. Editing it later doesn’t re-convert it at today’s rate, so last month’s budget never changes behind your back. To correct a rate, edit the transaction and change it. When you edit a transaction, Booked as shows what it counted for in your budget’s currency. Point at an amount in the register to see the same thing.

Transfers between currencies

When you transfer between two accounts in different currencies, enter the amount that leaves this account, then fill in Amount in (other currency): what arrived in the other account. The two sides count as the same value in your budget, so a transfer never changes Ready to Assign.

Importing

Bank statements for an account in another currency are read in that currency, and each transaction is converted at the latest rate you’ve entered. Enter at least one transaction (or the account’s starting balance) with a rate first.

Reports: spending and income

Choose Reports in the sidebar to see where your money went and how much you kept. There are two reports, on two tabs: Spending and Income vs expense.

Choosing the months

Both reports cover whole months. Pick a Range: this month, last month, the last 3, 6 or 12 months (counting this month), this year, or last year. Choose Custom… to pick the first and last month yourself. The months covered are shown next to the range.

Choose Chart or Table to switch between a chart and the same numbers as a table. The app remembers your choices until you quit.

Spending

Under Group by, choose Category or Payee.

By category. A ring chart and a ranked list show how much you spent in each category group, largest first, with each group’s share of the total. Choose a group to see its categories, then a category to see which payees the money went to. The path at the top (for example All groups › Food › Groceries) takes you back up a level.

By payee. The same chart and list show who you paid. The ten biggest payees get a slice each and the rest share one Other payees slice; choose it to list them. Choose a payee to see which categories its spending came from. Transactions without a payee are listed as No payee. Payees you’ve merged count as one.

Point at a slice to see its exact amount. Either way:

  • Spending is net. A refund back into a category reduces what that category (and that payee) spent.
  • Net inflow. If more came in than went out (for example, a gift you booked to a category, or a shop that refunded more than you spent there), it can’t be a slice of the chart. It’s listed under the chart as net inflow instead.
  • Uncategorised spending (imported transactions you haven’t given a category yet) has a row of its own.
  • Hidden categories and hidden payees still count.

The table view lists every group with its categories, or every payee with its categories.

Income vs expense

Totals for the months chosen (income, spending, net, and your savings rate, which is net as a share of income), then one pair of bars per month with net drawn as a line. The table view shows each month and a total row.

  • Income is money you put into Ready to Assign, including income held for next month. It counts in the month it arrived, not the month it’s budgeted in. A new account’s starting balance is income in the month you added the account, as it is in the budget.
  • Spending is the same net spending as on the Spending tab.

What counts

Only budget accounts count, and amounts are in your budget’s currency. Left out:

  • Transfers between your accounts, including paying off a credit card. That money was counted when you spent it.
  • A credit card’s starting debt, which was spent before you started the budget.
  • Tracking accounts.

Saving a report

Choose Export to CSV to save the report on screen for a spreadsheet:

  • Spending by category: one row per category, with its group, the amount spent and its share of all the spending in the chart.
  • Spending by payee: one row per payee and category, with the category’s group, the amount spent and its share of all the spending in the chart.
  • Income vs expense: one row per month and a total row.

Reports can’t be saved as pictures or PDFs.

Seeing your net worth

Your net worth is everything your accounts hold minus everything you owe on them. Choose Net worth in the sidebar to see it. The sidebar also shows the current total in small print next to Net worth.

What the page shows

  • Net worth, assets and debts today. An account with money in it counts as an asset. An account below zero counts as a debt. So an overdrawn current account is a debt, and a credit card you’ve overpaid is an asset.
  • Over time. One bar per month: assets above the line, debts below, with your net worth drawn as a line. Each month is valued on its last day, and the current month is valued today. Choose 12 months, 24 months or All; the page opens on your last choice. Point at a month to see exact amounts, or choose Show as a table to read the same numbers as a table.
  • By account type. Each type (current accounts, savings, credit cards, loans…) with its total, and each account’s value. Choose an account’s name to open its register.

Budget accounts and tracking accounts both count. Today’s figures include transactions dated in the future, so each account matches its balance in the sidebar. In the history, a future transaction counts from the month it’s dated. A closed account only appears if it still has a balance.

An investment or asset account with a value set counts at that value, plus the money paid in or taken out after it. See Investment and property values.

Accounts in other currencies

Accounts in another currency are converted into your budget’s currency using the exchange rates stored in the budget (see Accounts in other currencies). Each month uses the rate for that day: the latest rate on or before it, or the earliest one if all your rates are newer. The breakdown shows the account’s own balance and the rate used.

If a currency has no rate at all, those accounts are left out of the totals and a notice says so. Choose Add a rate to enter one.

Net worth only reads your accounts. Looking at it never changes your budget.

Investment and property values

The value of an investment or a house changes without any money moving. Use Set value to record what it is worth on a date. This works on investment and other asset accounts.

Setting a value

  1. Open the account and choose Set value.
  2. Enter the Date and the Value. The line beside them shows the account’s value on that date as things stand, or the value you are replacing if that date already has one.
  3. Choose Save value.

A value is the figure at the end of that day, so it includes that day’s transactions. Each date has one value, so setting a value for the same date again replaces the old one. Undo reverses it.

After that, the account is worth that value plus any money paid in or taken out after that date. For example, if you set your pension to £49,450.00 on 20 November and pay in £250.00 on 28 November, it is worth £49,700.00.

What changes

  • The top of the account shows Value and As of, the date of the latest value. If that date is more than a year ago, it says (over a year ago).
  • The sidebar, net worth (including its history) and the forecast use the value.
  • The register doesn’t change. Its Balance column still adds up the transactions, so it can differ from the value.

A value isn’t a transaction, so you can set one after the free trial ends.

Past values

Choose the As of date to see every value you have set, newest first. Edit opens Set value with that date and amount. Delete removes it. The account then goes back to the value before it, or to the total of its transactions if there is none.

Paying in and taking out

Keep entering real money as transactions on the account: contributions, employer payments and withdrawals. For a payment from your budget, enter a transfer from the budget account to the investment account and choose the category it comes from, such as Pension or Investing (see Transfers to and from tracking accounts). Money paid in from outside your budget, like an employer’s contribution, goes on the investment account with No category.

The forecast counts money paid into investment and asset accounts from outside your accounts as saving, and money taken out against it. A transfer from your budget is counted once, as part of your income minus spending. Opening balances and values don’t count.

Closing an asset account

An investment or asset account doesn’t need a zero balance to close. When you choose Close account, the app asks first. Its value becomes £0.00 from today, so it leaves your net worth from that date. Net worth before that date doesn’t change.

Past values shows the closing value with an Account closed tag. It has no Edit or Delete. Undo, or Reopen account, puts the value back.

Loans and mortgages

Debts don’t have a value to set. Enter the amount owed as the opening balance, then record payments as transfers into the loan or mortgage account. For a fixed loan, you can enter the total you will repay, interest included, as the opening balance, so the payments bring it to zero.

A house and its mortgage

Use two tracking accounts: a House (Other asset) and a Mortgage.

  • Buying. Record the loan as a transfer from the Mortgage account to the House account. For the deposit from a budget account, enter spending in a category such as House deposit, and the same amount coming into the House account with No category.
  • Valuing. Use Set value on the House account whenever you have an estimate.
  • Improvements. Enter money spent on an extension or a new kitchen as money paid into the House account. It adds to the value from that date.
  • Selling. Enter the sale money leaving the House account, then close it. Any amount left on the Mortgage account after the final payment is interest you didn’t record. Clear it with Reconcile.

Forecasting your net worth

The forecast projects your net worth 5, 10, 20 or 30 years ahead. Choose Forecast in the sidebar.

It uses your monthly saving and the growth rates under Assumptions.

The page

  • Net worth in the final year, with a low and a high figure, and your net worth today. Future amounts are rounded to whole units.
  • Monthly saving, with the income, spending and money into assets it comes from.
  • Net worth over time. The line is the expected value. The shaded band runs from poor years to good years. Point at the chart for a month’s amounts, or choose Show as a table for one row a year.
  • Milestones: the dates you pass round amounts such as £25,000, £50,000 and £100,000. If your net worth is below zero today, the first milestone is zero.
  • Goal: enter a net worth to reach. The forecast shows the expected date, plus the dates for good and poor years. Empty the box to remove the goal.

5 years, 10 years, 20 years and 30 years set how far ahead it goes. In today’s money removes inflation from future amounts.

How it’s calculated

The forecast starts from the balance of every open account today, including tracking accounts such as investments, pensions, property and loans. Then, for each month:

  1. Each account grows by its yearly rate divided by 12. An account grows only while its balance is above zero. A loan or mortgage adds interest only while money is owed.
  2. Scheduled transfers are made, such as payments into investments or towards a loan (see Scheduled transactions). Payments towards a debt stop when it reaches zero.
  3. The monthly saving is added to the chosen account. It rises with inflation each month, so it keeps its value.

Monthly saving is the average over the last 12 full months, or fewer for a newer budget, of your income minus your spending on budget accounts, plus money paid into investment and asset accounts from outside them, such as an employer’s pension contribution. Money taken out of them counts against it. Transfers between your accounts, opening balances and values you set are left out.

A pension contribution entered as a transfer from a budget account, with a category such as “Pension”, is counted once. It isn’t spending, and it’s already part of your income minus spending.

Each account starts from its value today. For an investment or asset account with a value set, the Value as of column shows that value’s date. See Investment and property values.

Assumptions

  • Monthly saving: your average. Choose Change to enter your own amount, and Use my average to go back.
  • Growth per year for each account. An empty box uses the default for its type: 2% for savings, 5% for investments, 3% for property and other assets, 0% for everything else. For a loan or mortgage, enter its interest rate. The table shows each account’s value today and in the final year. Saving goes here marks the account the monthly saving goes into. The default is your largest savings account.
  • Inflation per year. Monthly saving rises by this rate, and In today’s money uses it. Default 2.5%.

The good and poor years use each growing account’s rate plus and minus 2 percentage points. Debts and accounts at 0% stay the same.

Changes are saved in the budget. Undo reverses them.

What if…

A what-if shows a change next to the current forecast. A dashed line shows the forecast without it.

  • One-off: money out (a purchase) or money in on a date.
  • Monthly change: save more (or spend less), or save less (or spend more), each month from a date. It rises with inflation like the monthly saving.

What-ifs aren’t saved. They’re removed when you close the budget, or with Clear what-ifs.

Accounts in other currencies

Accounts in other currencies are converted at today’s exchange rate for the whole forecast. A currency with no rate is left out, and a notice says so. Choose Add a rate to enter one (see Accounts in other currencies).

Budget settings and passwords

Choose Settings at the bottom of the sidebar. Settings is split into sections, listed down the left: General, Security, Licence, Payees, Exchange rates, Export and backup, Appearance and Updates. Choose one to show it. In a narrow window the list becomes a Section dropdown. Settings opens on the section you last used.

General

  • Budget name: shown in the sidebar and window title. The file on disk keeps its original name. The new name is saved when you leave the field or press Enter; Esc puts the old name back.
  • Date format and Number format: how dates and amounts are shown and typed. Changing them doesn’t change any amounts.

Changes save as soon as you make them. Undo (⌘Z on a Mac, Ctrl+Z on Windows and Linux) reverses a settings change, like any other edit.

Below these you can see the budget’s currency (chosen when you created it) and where its file is.

Delete this budget

Delete budget… at the bottom of General moves this budget to the Trash and closes it, after you type its name (and its password, if it has one). See Delete a budget.

Security: passwords and auto-lock

A password encrypts the whole budget file. Without the password or the recovery key, nobody can open it, including us.

  • Add a password (budgets without one): type it twice. You’ll then see a recovery key. Copy it and keep it somewhere safe, such as a password manager or a printed copy in a drawer.
  • Change password: enter your current password and the new one twice. Your recovery key keeps working.
  • New recovery key: makes a new key and shows it once. The old key stops working, so replace any copy you kept.
  • Lock now: saves and closes the budget straight away.
  • Remove password: the file is no longer encrypted. Anyone with the file can read it.

Password changes are saved to the file immediately and can’t be undone.

Auto-lock: for a budget with a password, lock it after this many minutes without using the app. When it locks, the budget is saved and closed, and you’ll need the password (or recovery key) to open it again. It saves as soon as you pick it, and undo (⌘Z / Ctrl+Z) reverses it.

Forgot your password?

On the unlock screen, choose Use my recovery key instead and type the key. Then go to Settings → Security → Change password: you won’t be asked for the old password, so you can set a new one.

If you’ve lost both the password and the recovery key, the budget can’t be opened. There is no back door.

Appearance

Theme sets light or dark colours: Match my computer (the default, which follows your system’s light/dark setting), Light or Dark. It’s a setting for this computer and applies to every budget and the Welcome screen.

Format amounts as you type (on unless you turn it off) fills in amounts from the right and adds thousands separators as you type, so 123456 gives 1,234.56. Turn it off to type exactly what you press. It’s also a setting for this computer. See Typing amounts and sums.

Exporting and backing up

You can always get your data out. Export budget saves your whole budget in one plain-text file that you can keep as a backup, move to another computer, or read with your own tools.

Export your whole budget

  1. Open the budget and go to Settings → Export and backup.
  2. Choose Export budget….
  3. Pick a folder and file name, then save.

The file has everything in the budget: accounts, transactions (including splits and transfers), categories, assigned amounts, payees, flags, exchange rates and your budget’s settings. Undo history isn’t included, so a budget imported from the file starts with nothing to undo.

The export is written in one go: if something goes wrong part-way, any file that was already there is left as it was.

Password-protected budgets: the exported file is not encrypted, even if your budget has a password. Anyone who can open the file can read your finances, so store it somewhere safe. To keep an encrypted backup, copy the budget file itself instead.

Export to a spreadsheet (CSV)

In Settings → Export and backup → CSV for spreadsheets:

  • Transactions: every account or one account, for all dates or a period such as Last month or This year. Each line of a split transaction gets its own row (the Split column shows 1/2, 2/2 and so on). If any of the exported accounts is in another currency, two extra columns give each amount in your budget’s currency.
  • Budget: assigned, activity and available for every category, one row per category per month, for a range of months.

Amounts are plain numbers (no currency symbol or thousands separator) with the currency in its own column, so spreadsheets can add them up. Choose:

  • Separator: comma, semicolon or tab. If your spreadsheet expects a comma as the decimal separator, use semicolon.
  • Date format: ISO (2026-11-14, which every spreadsheet understands) or your budget’s own format.
  • For Excel on Windows: tick this if accented letters come out garbled when you open the file in Excel.

To export just some transactions (say, one payee or one month), search or filter a register and choose Export to CSV in its search row. See Searching and filtering.

Text that a spreadsheet might treat as a formula (starting with =, +, - or @) gets an apostrophe in front, so opening the file can never run anything.

Import an exported budget

  1. On the start screen, choose Import an exported budget….
  2. Pick the exported file.

This creates a new budget in your budgets folder, named after the exported budget, and opens it. It never changes an existing budget. If a budget with that name already exists, a number is added, for example Home (2). The new budget has no password; you can add one in Settings.

A file from an older version of the app imports normally and is brought up to date. A file from a newer version can’t be imported until you update the app.

If the file has been edited and no longer makes sense (for example, a transaction refers to an account that isn’t there), nothing is imported and you’ll see what was wrong.

For the technically curious

The file is UTF-8 JSON. It lists each table of the budget with its rows; amounts are whole numbers of the currency’s smallest unit (pence, cents) and dates are YYYY-MM-DD. Exporting the same budget twice gives an identical file, so exports work well with version control or file comparison tools.

Free trial and your licence

BudgetCoin comes with a 45-day free trial. Everything works during the trial: no features held back, no limits on budgets or accounts. The trial starts when you agree to the licence agreement, which the app shows the first time you open it.

During the trial

The bottom of the sidebar shows how many days are left. In the last week a reminder appears at the top of the screen, at 7 days, 3 days and on the last day. Close it with × and it stays closed until the next reminder. Reminders never appear during the setup tour.

You can also see the trial in Settings → Licence.

When the trial ends

Your budgets stay yours. Without a licence you can still:

  • open, view and search every budget
  • see reports and net worth
  • assign money and move it between categories
  • edit, delete and reconcile the transactions you already have
  • export everything (JSON and CSV) and back up your files

What stops is adding new transactions: the register’s entry row, importing bank files, new accounts with an opening balance, reconcile adjustments, and scheduled transactions being entered when they’re due (they wait and are entered as soon as you have a licence).

Buying a licence

Choose Buy in the sidebar, the reminder or Settings → Licence. After you pay, the licence key arrives by email, as text and as a .licence file.

A licence is perpetual: the version you buy keeps working forever.

  • One licence covers all your own computers.
  • It includes every update of that version: a BudgetCoin 1 licence covers 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 and so on.
  • The next major version (BudgetCoin 2) is a paid upgrade. If you bought within 90 days before it came out, it’s included.

Entering your licence

  1. Choose Enter licence (in the sidebar, in Settings → Licence, on the welcome screen, or in the app menu on a Mac).
  2. Paste the key from your email, or drop the .licence file onto the window (or use Choose file…).
  3. Choose Activate.

The app unlocks straight away, with no restart, no internet connection and nothing changed in your budgets. Nothing is sent anywhere: the key is checked on your computer.

If the key isn’t accepted, check that it was copied in full, from the first character to the last. Using the .licence file avoids copy mistakes. A key for an older major version is refused with a message saying which version it’s for.

Your licence in Settings

Remove licence from this computer… takes it off this computer, for example before you give the computer away. You can enter it again at any time; the key is in your purchase email.

Updating BudgetCoin

BudgetCoin checks once a day whether a newer version is out. When there is one, a notice appears at the top of the screen.

Installing a new version

  1. Choose Download in the notice. Our website’s download page opens in your browser.
  2. Download the installer for your computer and run it. It installs over the version you have.
  3. Open BudgetCoin again.

Your budgets are separate files, so installing a new version leaves them as they are. The app never downloads or installs anything by itself.

If you don’t want a particular version, choose Skip this version. You won’t hear about it again, but you will about the next one. × hides the notice until the next check.

New major versions

Your licence covers every release of its major version: a BudgetCoin 1 licence covers 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 and so on (see Free trial and your licence). When the next major version comes out, the notice says so and links to the website, where you can see what’s new and the upgrade price. The version you have keeps working.

If there’s also a newer release of the version you have, the notice offers that first.

Checking yourself

  • On a Mac: BudgetCoin → Check for Updates…
  • Anywhere: Settings → Updates → Check now

Settings → Updates also shows which version you have.

Turning the check off

Untick Check for updates automatically in Settings → Updates. The setting is for this computer and covers all your budgets.

The check reads one small file from our website with the latest version numbers. Nothing about you, your licence or your budgets is sent. See Privacy & security.

Entering dates

Every date field takes dates in your budget’s date format (Settings → General → Date format) and has a calendar.

Typing a date

Type the digits only: the separators are added for you. With the format DD/MM/YYYY, typing 15112026 gives 15/11/2026. Letters and other characters are ignored.

  • To finish a one-digit day or month early, type a separator: 1/2/2027 gives 01/02/2027.
  • Years always have four digits.
  • Only real dates are accepted. If you leave a field with an unfinished or impossible date (like 31/02/2027), it goes back to the date it had before.
  • ↑ and ↓ move the date a day later or earlier.

If your date format uses month names (like 15 Nov 2026), you type the date as numbers in day, month, year order (15/11/2026) and it is shown with the month name.

The calendar

Choose the calendar button at the end of the field, or press Alt+↓ while in the field.

  • Choose a day, or Today or Yesterday.
  • With the keyboard: the arrow keys move a day or a week, Page Up and Page Down move a month, Enter picks the highlighted day and Esc closes the calendar.

Weeks start on Monday, or on Sunday if your date format is month first (MM/DD/YYYY).

Typing amounts and sums

The decimal point and thousands separators go in for you

As you type digits into an amount, the last two become the pence (or cents). Type 1, 0, 1 and the box shows 0.01, then 0.10, then 1.01. So 1250 gives £12.50, and you never need to type the point for an everyday amount.

Thousands are separated as you go, in your budget’s number format: 123456 shows 1,234.56 (or 1.234,56, 1 234,56 or 1’234.56, depending on Settings → General).

  • To put the point somewhere else, type it yourself: 12.5 gives £12.50 and 50. gives £50.00.
  • Backspace removes the last digit: after 1250, it shows 1.25.
  • Pasted amounts are taken as they are.
  • Currencies without pence, like yen, only get the thousands separators: 12345 shows 12,345.
  • When you open an amount to change it, it’s shown with its separators, and typing at the end carries on in the same way.

This happens while you type at the end of the box, or over an amount you’ve selected. To type exactly what you press, turn off Format amounts as you type in Settings → Appearance.

Use your budget’s number format. In a budget set to 1.234,56, type 12,50. If something could be read two ways (like 12.5 in that format), the app asks you to check rather than guessing.

Maths is exact: 0.10 + 0.20 is always 0.30.

Privacy & security

  • Your budgets are files on your computer. Nothing is uploaded.
  • The app downloads two things. Exchange rates, only if you have accounts in other currencies: it fetches the European Central Bank’s public rates file, and you can turn this off in Settings → Exchange rates. And once a day it reads the latest version number from our website, which you can turn off in Settings → Updates. Nothing about you or your budget is sent with either.
  • A budget can be protected with a password (AES-256 encryption of the whole file).
  • A recovery key lets you get back in if you forget the password. Keep it somewhere safe. See Budget settings and passwords.
  • Without both the password and the recovery key, nobody can open the budget, including us.
